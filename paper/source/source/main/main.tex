\title{Which Winning Continuations Survive One-Way Helper Encodings?}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous authors}}
\begin{document}\raggedbottom\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
A controller adapting to changed requirements must often continue from its
current state, with obligations still pending. A negative synthesis answer
can exclude a viable continuation if the encoding used for the query loses
source-winning states. We characterize exactly what a one-way Boolean-helper
encoding used in Kind synthesis preserves under strict semantics. Its added
input permits a previously forbidden environment move one step before
releasing the controller's safety duty. A continuation survives exactly when
one winning source strategy keeps a safe response to the helper's first
change available for as long as the helper may wait.
This gives a necessary and sufficient whole-region preservation condition
and an extension for translations that retain safety duties after the change.
A constructed Patrol update shows why being able to respond now is insufficient:
a source-winning continuation can lose that ability while meeting its charging
obligation. Released Vehicle and Obstacle inputs distinguish initialization,
retained-state preservation and public tool answers. At one fixed Obstacle query, releasing only the first-change system duty
changes the same solver's Boolean answer from false to true. Accompanying executable checks make the case analyses inspectable. The results establish when preservation can
support candidate exclusion; they do not establish an operational gate benefit.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
self-adaptive systems, reactive synthesis, continuation preservation,
